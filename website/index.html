The world's first native screen extender for Linux.

Your Android phone or tablet becomes a real second monitor. Not a web page in a browser tab: your GPU encodes the screen and the phone's hardware decodes it, so a window you drag across keeps up with your hand.

Install on Linux Download the APK

Install with one command on Ubuntu, Fedora or Arch:

$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/saharshbaiju/tandem/main/install.sh | bash

Not a mirror.

Not a browser tab.

A real monitor.

Your compositor gets a new display, so windows, workspaces and scaling work like on any monitor you plug in. The phone shows it full screen, frame by frame, the moment each one arrives.

From the moment a pixel changes on your computer to the moment it lights up on the phone. Typically 25 to 45 ms over USB, with no buffering anywhere in between.

Three ways to plug in

USB cable

The fastest. Turn on USB debugging once and plug in; Tandem sets up the link by itself. Works even when the phone is on mobile data.lowest latency

USB tethering

Same cable, no developer options. Switch on USB tethering in the phone's hotspot settings and the computer finds it.no setup on the phone

Wi-Fi

No cable at all. Put both on the same network. If a firewall blocks the phone, the live view tells you and opens port 5000 when you press f.cable-free

One command.
That's the install.

$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/saharshbaiju/tandem/main/install.sh | bash

Paste it into a terminal on Ubuntu, Fedora or Arch. It works out what your desktop is missing, asks for your password once, and adds the tandem command. Nothing to compile, nothing to configure. Run it again to update.

  1. Run the command

    The one above, in any terminal. Here's what it prints: about a minute on a fresh system, seconds when you update.

  2. Start Tandem

    Open a new terminal, run tandem and pick Start Tandem. A QR code appears with the computer's addresses and a pairing code.

    While it waits, the live view checks the cable, the firewall and Wi-Fi, and says why a phone can't connect yet.

  3. Install the app on the phone

    Download the APK from the latest release on the phone and open it. Android 8 or newer. Each phone maker words the permission a little differently:

    Download the APK

    Had it before as Screen Extender? Tandem installs as a separate app, so uninstall the old one.

  4. Scan and connect

    In the app tap Scan QR code, point it at the terminal and tap Connect. Ripples spread from your finger, then the screen dissolves into your extended desktop.

    The phone's own camera app works too: the code opens Tandem and asks you to confirm the computer.

    Touch the phone's screen to use it. On GNOME there's nothing to set up. On Hyprland, touch needs a one-time permission: the installer offers it, or press t in the live view.

Only your phone can connect

The QR code is the key

It carries a random pairing key and the fingerprint of the computer's certificate. Typing an address instead? Enter the pairing code too.

Both sides prove it

Nothing reaches a phone until the phone and the computer have each shown they know the key.

Everything is encrypted

Video and touches travel over TLS. Others on the same Wi-Fi can neither watch nor touch.

Runs where you work

HyprlandArch, or anything running Hyprland. A headless output becomes the phone's monitor, encoded with NVENC or VA-API.
GNOME on WaylandUbuntu 22.04+, Fedora 36+, any GNOME 40+. Mutter makes the virtual monitor, PipeWire and GStreamer carry it.
Android 8+Phones, tablets and foldables. The app decodes in hardware and draws each frame the moment it arrives.
